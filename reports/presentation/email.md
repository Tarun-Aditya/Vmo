**Subject:** Contract Intelligence for Property Managers

Hi,

Lately I've been working on a concept: a data pipeline that takes unstructured information, such
as documents, PDFs and scans, and turns it into structured data that systems and people can act on.
I've applied the same idea, at scale, to the supplier contracts behind our hotel operations and our
property managers.

Every hotel in our group runs on supplier contracts, for cleaning, food, waste, maintenance,
parking and software, and every property manager has to keep track of them. Today the terms that
matter sit inside PDFs and scans. Renewal deadlines, notice periods, liability limits and payment
terms are found by hand, one document at a time, at each hotel, and are often found too late.

With agentic AI, agents pick up each contract as it lands, read it, and pull out its dates, terms,
obligations and risks as structured, linked records. From then on we're working with data, not
documents: the property manager knows what to act on this week, and an owner sees the same picture
across every hotel.

**Manual way vs agentic way:**

- **Reading:** a person reads every page of every contract → agents read each file as it arrives,
  including scans.
- **Capturing terms:** copied into spreadsheets, differently at each hotel → stored once, in one
  structure, with the page each value came from.
- **Amendments:** filed separately, and the old terms are still followed → applied to the original,
  so the current terms are always clear.
- **Renewals:** calendar reminders, if someone set them → every last day to act is worked out and
  raised ahead of time.
- **Checking terms:** ad hoc, by whoever has time → every contract checked against our standards,
  with risks flagged.
- **Questions across hotels:** days of searching folders → one question, answered in seconds.
- **Scale:** more contracts means more people → ten contracts or ten thousand, the same process.

**Why it pays back:**

- **Time:** reading and logging one contract by hand takes about [1 hour]. Across [N] contracts and
  [N] hotels, that is [N] days a year handed back to property managers.
- **Renewals we didn't mean to make:** if the notice date passes unnoticed, the contract renews
  itself, often for years and at higher prices [in our sample, another 36 months with rates rising
  at least 4% a year]. Stopping even one unwanted renewal a year covers the cost.
- **Money left on the table:** terms we've negotiated but don't apply, such as [an amendment that
  moved payment from 45 to 30 days], rate caps or service credits, become visible and enforceable.
- **Risk:** uncapped liability, missing insurance and missing data-protection terms are flagged
  across the group, not discovered after a claim.

I'd like to show you how it works. In 20 minutes:

- **One contract:** watch an agent read a signed PDF, split it into clauses, find each term in its
  sentence, and store it as rows in a database, with every value traced back to its page.
- **A folder of ten:** mixed PDFs, Word files and a scan become the same rows. The agents link
  related files, apply an amendment to the terms it changes, and turn away a duplicate.
- **What the rows tell us:** questions that take days by hand, answered in a second.
