Teal steps run on the data automatically. Rust steps keep a person’s sign-off. Each renewal feeds the next draft: what we agreed, what we paid, what went wrong.

The idea is not only about reading old contracts. The same data serves the whole lifecycle. A new contract is drafted from approved clauses. A vendor's draft is redlined against our playbook. Approval is routed by value and risk. The signed copy is read back into data, linked to its master agreement. From then on it is tracked: obligations, service levels, invoices, deadlines. And renewal is decided before the notice date with the full history. The teal steps are automated on the data; the rust steps keep a human sign-off, which is a deliberate trade-off: structure adds review points where judgement matters.
