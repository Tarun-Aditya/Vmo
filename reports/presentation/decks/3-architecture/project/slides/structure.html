Structure is what turns a wall of text into something a system can reason about. First the text is split into clauses at its headings and numbering, each clause keeping its page. Then each clause is recognised as one of 44 key clause types, such as renewal, liability cap, termination for convenience, insurance, data protection, payment or service levels, using its heading, keywords and wording. The library of clause types is configuration: an administrator can add types, keywords and aliases. A clause buried inside another, for example a termination right in a general clause, is found by sentence, and with Claude on, AI tags what the rules miss. Then documents are linked: a schedule that says it is issued under a master agreement of a given date is attached to it, and an amendment is attached to what it amends and overrides the terms it changes. The same step reads the supplier's name, the document type, the title, the hotel or site, and who signed and when.
