THE RESULT: ONE INTERNAL FORM

A title, a heading per clause, page markers, and normalised wording (“thirty (30) days” → 30 days, “US$” → USD), so every later step sees the same shape whatever the source.

Reading turns any file into one internal text form. PDFs are read with their layout, and tables laid out in columns are rebuilt into rows; each page is marked so every later citation can name its page. A scanned PDF has no text layer: its pages are rendered and read by OCR with Tesseract. Word files keep their headings and tables; Word has no fixed pages, so the system does not invent page numbers for them. Text and HTML, including email bodies, are read as they are. The result is the same shape for every source: a title, a heading per clause, page markers, and normalised wording, for example “thirty (30) days” becomes 30 days and “US$” becomes USD, so the next steps never need to know where a document came from.
