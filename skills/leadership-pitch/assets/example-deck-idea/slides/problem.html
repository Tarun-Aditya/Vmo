THE PROBLEM

Our business runs on contracts, but their terms live in documents

Locked in files

Dates, prices, renewal rules, obligations and risks sit in PDFs, Word files, scans and email attachments across drives.

Manual, one at a time

For every contract someone drafts, compares, checks, keys terms into a spreadsheet and sets reminders by hand.

Fine for a few

It works for a handful of contracts. It breaks at enterprise scale: thousands of documents, many teams, constant change.

Misses cost money

A renewal window passes, a cap is missing, an obligation is forgotten. Each is in the document; nobody saw it in time.

The information exists. It just isn’t data.

So it cannot be searched, joined, counted, tracked or alerted on.

The point of this slide: the problem is not missing information. Everything we need is written in the contract. The problem is form: it is in documents, so every process around a contract is manual and runs one document at a time. Renewals, obligations, redlines, new drafts, tracking, alerts: each is a person reading. That is fine for ten contracts and impossible for ten thousand. Example to tell: a waste hauler's contract renews for three years unless cancelled by certified mail in a 90-day window; the window closed and nobody noticed. The clause was there all along.
